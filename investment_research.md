## 1. Executive summary

On has a credible pathway to become significantly larger over a 5–10-year horizon. Its global premium brand, innovation-led footwear franchise, direct-to-consumer (DTC) distribution and strong Asia-Pacific momentum are the core drivers. However, high expectations are already embedded in growth-stock valuations. The main question is not just whether revenue compounds, but **whether it compounds profitably without sacrificing full-price discipline**—and whether a future investor will still pay a premium equity revenue multiple.

On reported **CHF 3.014bn of FY2025 revenue**, up **30.0%**, with a **62.8% gross profit margin**, **18.8% adjusted EBITDA margin** and DTC accounting for **41.8%** of sales [1]. Q2 2026 revenue grew **13.5% as reported** and **21.6% in constant currency**, to CHF 850.3m. Q2 DTC grew **26.0%**, APAC **43.1%**, but Americas only **4.5%** and wholesale **4.8%** in reported CHF [2]. Management guided full-year 2026 reported revenue to **CHF 3.47bn–3.56bn**, at least **65.0% gross margin**, and a **19.5–20.0% adjusted EBITDA margin** [2]. Constant-currency growth is not the same as revenue growth in reported CHF.

## 2. Business model and industry context

On sells premium performance footwear, apparel and accessories. Its purpose-built performance innovations include CloudTec and LightSpray. Manufacturing is substantially outsourced. On sells through **wholesale partners** (sports retailers and specialty shops) and **DTC** (its owned e-commerce and retail stores). For 2025, approximately **93% of sales came from shoes**, so a broader 'head-to-toe' sportswear strategy remains unproven in absolute scale [1].

### Three long-run pillars

**Premium product and brand:** Protecting product differentiation and full-price selling may preserve strong gross profit margins and brand value. **DTC:** E-commerce and owned retail can improve customer proximity and reduce reliance on retail intermediaries, but expanding a store network entails cost, lease commitments and inventory. **Global reach:** On has an established Americas base and an expanding EMEA/APAC business. Fast APAC growth could reduce concentration over time, although the smaller base makes percentage growth easier.

**Competition:** On competes for discretionary footwear budgets against large global athletic footwear players and specialized running brands. Price premiums are not permanent barriers. The model does not claim a quantified addressable market or rival multiples without verified comparable filings.

## 4. What Q2 2026 tells us

The headline **13.5%** reported growth and **21.6%** constant-currency growth are both correct [2]. Constant-currency reporting retranslates current-period sales at prior-year rates and adjusts for FX translation; it does **not** mean that reported sales grew by 21.6%.

The most promising datapoint is DTC +26.0% (constant currency +34.3%), at CHF 388.4m or **45.7% of quarterly sales**. APAC +43.1% reported is also important. In contrast, Americas +4.5% and wholesale +4.8% reported temper the growth story. Q2 gross margin increased to 65.4%, while adjusted EBITDA margin was 19.8%. The 2026 guidance remains a company forecast, not a completed year [2].

**Illustrative calculation, 2031 Base:** Sales CHF 7,371.7m × P/S 3.0 = CHF 22,115.2m of equity value; divided by 336.109m approximate diluted Class A economic-equivalent shares = CHF 65.80 per A share; divide by CHF 0.8270 per USD, then discount five years at 11% → about **USD 47.22**. A P/S equity multiple already includes net cash considerations embedded in the market multiple: **do not add net cash again**.

On has two share classes. Per the company's SEC disclosures, Class B has one-tenth of the Class A capital/economic basis, and 10 Class B may convert into one Class A [1,3]. The model uses June 30, 2026 outstanding Class A (301.715535m), Class B (324.991680m) and reported dilutive awards; this is a simplified fully diluted estimate, not a contemporaneous actual market capitalisation.

**Valuation is extremely sensitive** to a terminal P/S and discount rate; see Excel `Sensitivity` sheet. A premium company can create operating value for 10 years without the shareholder getting an exceptional rate of return if a lower multiple is paid at exit.

## 7. Investment recommendation & catalysts

**Educational conclusion: HOLD / WATCHLIST at USD 35.41 (19 Oct 2026 dated reference).** I would want stronger proof that DTC momentum can coexist with healthy wholesale orders, good inventory conversion and durable margins before treating today's premium product narrative as a high-conviction equity opportunity. A more attractive valuation or a demonstrated acceleration in Americas results could change that stance. This is **not personal financial advice**.

**Watch for positive evidence:** DTC sales and full-price rates remain robust; APAC grows despite a higher base; apparel becomes material; sustainable EBIT and operating cash flow expansion. **Watch for negative evidence:** regional slowing, weaker orders from wholesale partners, elevated discounts, rising inventory, rising customer acquisition expenses, margin compression, FX headwinds and persistent divergence between earnings and cash.

## 8. Key risks and explicit model limitations

1. **Multiple compression:** Even strong 2036 revenues may be paired with a much lower exit price-to-sales multiple.
2. **Competition and consumer demand:** Premium sportswear is discretionary; competitors can respond through product launches and promotions.
3. **Shoes concentration:** The 2025 business relied heavily on footwear (approximately 93% of revenue); apparel and accessories are growing from small bases.
4. **Geography and FX:** Earning foreign currency but reporting CHF causes material FX translation effects. Q2 2026 reported and constant-currency growth diverged notably.
5. **Wholesale and inventory:** Distributor-order timing, returns, price discipline and working capital may create cash-flow volatility.
6. **Share structure and dilution:** Different Class A/B economics and future share awards complicate conventional per-share valuation.
7. **Valuation-method limitations:** The project is a P/S teaching cross-check, not a fundamental DCF. Exit P/S is assumed; no forecasting of interest, taxes, working capital, cash flows, lease liabilities, discount rate term structure, exchange rates, future dilution or distributions. Current share count is held flat.
